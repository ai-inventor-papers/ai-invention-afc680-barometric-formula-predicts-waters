## Layman Summary

Computes how accurately three simple formulas predict the temperature at which water boils at altitudes from sea level to 4000 m, measured against a standard high-accuracy water property equation.

## Full Summary

This deterministic CPU experiment screens three closed-form methods for the boiling temperature of water at altitude: constant-latent-heat Clausius-Clapeyron anchored at the normal boiling point (373.124 K), temperature-dependent-latent-heat Clausius-Clapeyron integrated from the same anchor using IAPWS-IF97 enthalpies, and the Antoine equation with NIST WebBook water constants refitted to the reference over 273.15-373.15 K. Each is inverted for temperature on an 81-point grid from 0 to 4000 m (50 m steps), with pressure from the U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 troposphere formula. The reference is the IAPWS-IF97 saturation curve (region 4), taken through the iapws package 1.5.5 and checked at the triple point (611.657 Pa) and the normal boiling point (101325 Pa within 5 Pa). Metrics are maximum absolute error, RMS error, altitude of the maximum, bias and error at 4000 m. Survival rule: maximum absolute error at or below 0.6 K; survivors are ranked by RMS. Headline results: the Antoine refit has maximum error 0.0042 K (RMS 0.0022 K); temperature-dependent latent heat has 0.177 K maximum (RMS 0.108 K); constant latent heat has 0.278 K maximum (RMS 0.152 K), growing with altitude. A sensitivity row with the grid-mean latent heat (not ranked) reaches 0.407 K. The Clapeyron consistency check at 1 percent fails (maximum 1.59 percent), which the ideal-vapour factor 1/Z_v explains; this is reported, not corrected. No field records or published boiling-point measurements are used, so the comparison is against the reference equation only. Headline numbers were re-derived in pure Python with a separate equation of state (IAPWS-95) in rederive_headlines.py.
