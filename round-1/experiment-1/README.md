## Layman Summary

We compare two simple air-pressure formulas that predict how the boiling point of water falls with altitude, checking each against a standard atmosphere reference from 0 to 4,000 metres, without any field measurements.

## Full Summary

This artifact provides a reference-grid screen of two barometric pressure formulas for the boiling point of water from 0 to 4,000 m in 100 m steps. The isothermal form (single scale height, about 8,435 m) and the standard lapse-rate form are each converted to a boiling temperature using the Wagner-Pruss (IAPWS) saturation equation, inverted by bisection. The reference boiling temperature uses U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 pressures, which the lapse-rate form reproduces to within 0.01 percent at the tabulated nodes. Sea-level check: 99.974 C at 101.325 kPa. Results: the isothermal structural term (isothermal minus lapse-rate boiling temperature) is 0.04 K at 1 km, 0.15 K at 2 km, 0.33 K at 3 km and 0.59 K at 4 km. Against the reference, the isothermal form has max absolute error 0.588 K at 4,000 m and RMS 0.269 K, a margin of 0.012 K below the 0.6 K limit, so the verdict is borderline and no pass or fail claim is made. The lapse-rate form is the reference itself, so its error is zero by construction. No field measurements, humidity or weather terms are used; this is a model-to-reference screen and must not be presented as validation against measured boiling points. Downstream use: the structural term and max/RMS errors can be cited as the model-structure contribution; any claim about measured boiling points requires separate data. Key files: method.py, results/grid_values.csv, results/summary.json, results/method_out.json and its full/mini/preview variants at the workspace root. An independent recomputation with the iapws package reproduces the isothermal errors to 0.00002 K.
