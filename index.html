Barometric Boiling Formula: Structural Error at 4000 m

The question: how well does a simple barometric formula predict the boiling point of water at altitude, compared with published measurements? What we found: no measured boiling record could be verified, so the comparison with measurement is untested. Against the IAPWS industrial reference saturation curve, the isothermal barometric form adds a structural error of 0.588 K at 4000 m relative to the lapse-rate form. That is 0.088 K above a 0.5 K structural target fixed before the calculation. It is one exploratory screen, with no interval and no measured data.

Method

The method asks one narrow question: how much does the choice of barometric model shift a boiling estimate, once the saturation equation is held fixed? Altitude goes in, and a boiling temperature comes out for each of two pressure models. Their difference is the structural term.

  1. Input. An altitude z from 0 to 4000 m, evaluated at 100 m steps on the reference grid.
  2. Two pressure models. The isothermal model uses a single scale height, p = p0 exp(−z/H) with H = 8434.5 m. The lapse-rate model follows the troposphere relation with L = 0.0065 K/m and T0 = 288.15 K. Its nodes match the tabulated Standard Atmosphere values to within 0.01 percent.
  3. Pressure to boiling temperature. Each pressure is inverted to a boiling temperature Tb on the Wagner-Pruss (IAPWS-95) saturation equation by bisection, with the inverse checked by round trip. At 101325 Pa the sea-level check gives 373.1243 K.
  4. Output. The structural term s(z) = Tb,iso(z) − Tb,lapse(z). The saturation equation cancels in this difference, so the term reflects the barometric model alone.

The vapour-pressure routes are compared separately. Three closed-form inversions run on a 50 m grid from 0 to 4000 m: constant-latent-heat Clausius-Clapeyron, temperature-dependent-latent-heat Clausius-Clapeyron, and a three-parameter Antoine equation. The Antoine constants are fitted on 273.15 to 363.15 K, and points from 363.15 to 373.15 K are held out.

Results

Structural term against altitude

On the shared reference grid, measured against the lapse-rate form and the 0.5 K structural target, the isothermal structural term grows with altitude. The target is missed at 4000 m. The term crosses the target only between 3000 m and 4000 m. The paper treats this as a property of the model, so it holds whether or not a measured comparison is ever run.

The reference is the iapws Python package, version 1.5.5, checked at the triple point and the normal boiling point. No measured boiling records enter the reference.

Vapour-pressure routes, held-out error

On the 60 held-out points, the refitted Antoine equation has the lowest RMS error, ahead of temperature-dependent-latent-heat Clausius-Clapeyron and constant-latent-heat Clausius-Clapeyron. On the full grid, the constant-latent-heat route reaches 0.280 K at 4000 m, and the temperature-dependent route reaches 0.176 K at 4000 m. These are reference-only, exploratory estimates.

The Antoine ranking is provisional. An in-sample RMS of 0.0042 K for the refit could not be reproduced, and repeating the fit gives 0.036 K. A sensitivity fit on 273.15 to 353.15 K has a held-out RMS of 0.092 K on a different 81-point set (353.15 to 373.15 K), so it is not ranked and cannot be set against the 60-point figures; the ranking's stability under that window is untested.

Measured comparison

A measured record needed station pressure, elevation, observed boiling temperature and thermometer uncertainty, with at least 15 accepted sites for a claim. The search ran eight broad web queries and one dataset hub. Zero records met all four criteria. No predicted-minus-observed residual was computed, and the verdict on the 0.6 K total error bound is absent.

Limitations

  • The central limitation is that no measured boiling record was verified. The paper answers how the barometric formula departs from a reference equation, not how it departs from measured boiling points, which is the question asked.
  • The headline is a single screen on one shared reference grid, with no interval. It was not registered in advance as a test, and its 0.5 K target is a structural threshold, not a measured tolerance.
  • The structural term is computed on one reference pressure model. The lapse-rate form follows the Standard Atmosphere 1976 troposphere, and the primary standard document was not read directly, so its provenance is secondary. Its values agree with the tabulated nodes, so the effect on the result is small.
  • The Antoine ranking is provisional. Its stability under a second fit range is untested, and the in-sample value did not reproduce on repetition. The Clapeyron check is largely an identity, because the same reference equations supply its inputs. The 0.6 K total bound is untested.
  • The study covers one altitude range, 0 to 4000 m, and one water-only chemistry. Humidity, weather-driven pressure changes and dissolved solutes are excluded.
